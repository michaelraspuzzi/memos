# Founder Memo

Write for a founder seeking a decision, not a reader collecting company facts. The memo should make the business legible, tell one causal story, and end with actions that can be applied at comparable company stages.

## Build the argument

1. **Choose one tension.** State the company’s strategic problem in one sentence: distribution versus control, growth versus margin, focus versus expansion, or another specific tradeoff. Use it as the narrative spine and remove later restatements.
2. **Explain the product in economic terms.** Describe what the customer receives, what difficult work the product replaces, who operates it, and where money or recurring effort flows. Include technical detail only when it changes the business conclusion.
3. **Show value through customers.** Lead with two cases that demonstrate different buying motions at meaningful scale. Compress minor examples into supporting evidence. Distinguish company-reported results from audited data.
4. **Tell the change chronologically.** Show why the original strategy worked, what new evidence exposed its limit, what management changed, when users felt the change, and what the response cost. Integrate dates into the story; use a timeline only when it adds information.
5. **Analyze alternatives.** Explain the path chosen and the organizations required by credible alternatives. Use the closest business-model comparator, which may differ from the most visually similar product.
6. **Test whether it worked.** Use revenue, adoption, headcount, retention, pricing, and product releases over time. Separate leading indicators from business outcomes and identify the decisive undisclosed metric.
7. **End with founder actions.** For each relevant phase, specify an action, a test or metric, and the case evidence supporting it. Advice such as “build community” or “find a business model” fails unless it says what to do next.

## Default structure

- One bold position paragraph followed by four or five numbered claims with figures.
- Product and economic mechanism.
- Customer value, with asymmetric case-study depth.
- The turning point told as a sequence.
- The response, tradeoffs, comparator, and new risks.
- Evidence of results, with numbers over time.
- Future scenarios and the metrics that distinguish them.
- An actionable founder playbook.
- Sources and research notes.

Adapt the structure to the story. Keep a typical memo near 2,500 words before sources unless the user requests another length.

## Research and presentation rules

- Prefer primary sources: company announcements, product and license documentation, repositories, APIs, filings, and direct customer studies. Use secondary reporting for reaction, disputed history, or unavailable private-company facts; label its limitations.
- Verify current pricing, licenses, leadership, metrics, and product claims at writing time. Mark inference explicitly.
- Round figures in prose, use exact values in tables, state each important number once, and never treat downloads, stars, or logos as revenue.
- Include product screenshots and a time-series chart when they materially improve understanding. Caption the inference each visual supports and name the source.
- Use claim-style headers that let a reader recover the argument by skimming.
- Avoid duplicate summaries, fourth-wall notes about prior drafts, equal-length case studies, and repeated “not X, but Y” constructions.
- Preserve uncertainty. “The company has not disclosed renewal” is analysis; inventing a proxy is not.

## Quality eval

Score the finished memo out of 100. A memo is publishable at **85 or above**, with no hard-gate failure.

| Dimension | Points | Full-credit standard |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Thesis and narrative | 20 | One tension drives a chronological, causal story; conclusions follow the evidence and are not repeated. |
| Product and economics | 15 | A founder can explain the product, buyer, avoided cost, value flow, and monetization constraint after one read. |
| Customer evidence | 15 | Two strong cases prove distinct buying motions; minor examples are compressed and claims are caveated. |
| Research integrity | 15 | Material claims are traceable, current facts are verified, primary sources dominate, and inference is labeled. |
| Quantitative discipline | 10 | Numbers are consistent, time-based, properly qualified, and connected to a business conclusion. |
| Founder usefulness | 20 | Lessons identify phase-specific actions, tests, metrics, failure modes, and the evidence behind them. |
| Editing and presentation | 5 | The memo is near target length, visually useful, skimmable by headers, and free of structural repetition. |

Hard gates:

- The memo has a declared founder audience and does not drift into an undeclared investment recommendation.
- Every decisive factual claim has a source or an explicit uncertainty label.
- The conclusion contains at least three concrete founder actions with corresponding tests or metrics.
- Requested visuals, code, or format are either delivered or explicitly ruled out because they conflict with the chosen audience.
- No unsupported revenue, funding, customer, or adoption figure survives the final pass.

After drafting, report the score by dimension and revise every dimension below 80% of its available points. Deliver the memo only after rerunning the hard gates and checking the generated artifact, links, and visuals.
