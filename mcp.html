MCP gave AI a socket. The hard part is deciding what to plug in.

Three three-minute films on the Model Context Protocol: what it is, how to build a server worth using, and whether your company should have one at all

October 14, 2026 · Michael Raspuzzi · 4 min read · 9 min of film

The CEO Summary

  • MCP is a socket, not a smarter model. It turns M×N custom integrations into M+N, and OpenAI, Google and Microsoft adopted it within six months.
  • The code is 20 lines. The design is the job. 97% of real tool descriptions have a flaw. Build for the task, not your API.
  • Strategy comes before servers. If your product is a verb, ship wide. If it's a noun, open a narrow window.

01The brain in a jar got a socket

The smartest AI in the world can write you a sonnet, but ask whether you need an umbrella and it has no idea. It's a brain in a jar.

The old fix was custom wiring: three apps and four tools meant twelve integrations. That's how you end up with a drawer full of chargers.

Anthropic proposed MCP in November 2024: a USB-C port for AI, so M×N becomes M+N.1 A host, like Claude or your code editor, talks to servers: small programs that wrap a tool. The conversation is almost boring. "What can you do?" "get_weather, give me a location." "London." "Rain by noon." Take the umbrella.

A year in: 10,000+ public servers, 97M monthly SDK downloads, and a handoff to the Linux Foundation.2 Two catches. On a benchmark of real MCP tasks, GPT-5 finished under 44%.3 And a tool's description is text the model trusts, so 5.5% of 1,899 servers studied were poisoned.4

MCP solved the wiring. It didn't solve judgment, and it gave attackers a new front door.

02Twenty lines of code, a hundred decisions

Building a server is almost insultingly easy. Put @mcp.tool on a Python function: the type hints become the schema, the docstring becomes what the model reads. Then most people ruin it by mirroring their API (list_users, list_events), and the model burns thousands of tokens to find one free half hour. A study of 856 real tools found flaws in 97% of their descriptions.5

The fixes read like advice for onboarding a new hire:6

RuleIn practice
Design for the taskOne schedule_event beats three endpoints
Names are promptscalendar_search, never just search
Return what a human would readAnthropic's Slack example: 206 tokens down to 72
Fail helpfully"Start time must be in the future" lets the model retry
Label what's dangerousMark "cancel" destructive, so the app asks you first
Keep the menu shortOne setup loaded 150,000 tokens of tools before doing anything

A good server isn't a mirror of your API. It's a tool you'd hand a new colleague, with instructions they can't misread.

03Is your product a verb or a noun?

800M people use ChatGPT weekly, and apps now live inside it, built on MCP. Awkward question for every founder: what happens when the user never opens your app?

Figma and Canva went wide. Figma hands coding agents the real design, so every agent makes Figma more of the source of truth. Canva makes the poster inside the chat. Both sell the making of things, and being where making starts is distribution.7

Substack opened a crack. It sells the writing, so an AI summarizing every post for free is a leak, not a channel. Its server is for writers only. The stats go out to your AI. The writing stays home.8

Verbs (design, book, send, pay) gain value when agents can do them. Nouns (the article, the community, data only you have) are worth guarding.

The Bottom Line

MCP doesn't decide what kind of company you are. It just makes you answer sooner. Before writing server code, ask: Who still sees the user once your app becomes a back end? What could an attacker make your server do? And does all that agent usage ever turn into revenue?
